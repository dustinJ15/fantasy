# Research report: what makes redraft trade offers get accepted (and how to encode it)

Scope note: this is web research only; no code was touched. Two of the three requested empirical sources do not exist in findable form: I could not locate any published dataset or study giving **acceptance rates by offer type** (1-for-1 vs 2-for-1, by position, etc.), and reddit.com is blocked to the search tool, so no r/fantasyfootball "I tracked N offers" posts could be verified. Everything below that is quantitative is from analysts' stated rules, tool documentation, or behavioral-economics literature, and is cited as such. Where a number is a rule of thumb rather than data, I say so.

The two failing offers in the prompt map directly onto the most-repeated rejection causes in the literature: (a) asking a manager for his **only starter at a position** (Montgomery for Dak, single-QB roster) and (b) a **2-for-1 for the best player at a position** with no premium and no fit (McMillan + bench WR for Chase). Both are the "RB2 + WR3 for their RB1" offer PFF says managers "scoff at."

---

## 1. What actually gets a redraft trade accepted

**Base rate is low, even for experts.** PFF's Jeff Ratcliffe, who completes more trades than almost anyone in his leagues, says "only about one in 30 of my trade offers are accepted" and calls that "par for the course once you take into account the inherent biases" ([PFF, Trade Strategy Part 2](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-2/)). Most leagues see very few trades: an FFToday reader survey found leagues where the top seed "had 1 of the 5 total league trades," "no trades in our league," and "half of league has none"; the only pattern was that top seeds traded at or above league average ([FFToday](https://fftoday.com/articles/davis/12_q&a_wk16_traders_winners.html)). Implication for an algorithm: the prior on any single offer is ~3–5%, so an offer generator should optimize for *not* burning credibility, and send offers that survive the recipient's first-glance heuristics.

**The recipient's first-glance heuristics, in order of how often sources name them:**

1. **"Did I get the best player in the deal?"** theScore: "Nine times out of 10, the owner getting the best player in the deal usually wins the trade" and "always value quality over quantity when it comes to multi-player trades" ([theScore](https://www.thescore.com/news/1056175/building-a-winning-fantasy-team-through-trades)). 4for4's playoff-prep piece: "The winner in a trade this time of year is whoever gets the best player… Studs win championships -- not depth" ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2016/w10/gearing-fantasy-playoffs)). Bleacher Report's cardinal rule: "DON'T TAKE A 2 FOR 1 TRADE, UNLESS YOU'RE GETTING THE 1" ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/394608-master-the-art-of-the-2-for-1-trade)). This is the lens through which the Chase offer was read: the recipient gives the best player, so he assumes he lost.

2. **Does it move points from my starting lineup to my bench?** PFF: "If your offer moves points from their starting lineup to their bench, then don't bother," and "Don't be surprised when they scoff at a two-for-one of your RB2 and WR3 for their RB1" ([PFF Part 1](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-1/)). PFF tip #23: "To trade away your depth for a stud, you have to flood your trade partner's starting lineup with significant value" ([PFF, 30 Ways](https://www.pff.com/news/30-ways-to-win-trade)). The Fantasy Footballers: "pull up their roster and pretend it's your roster," and "Why would the Bell owner accept a trade that returns two balls of pocket lint, a paper clip, and a used tissue?" ([Fantasy Footballers, 10 tips](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/fantasy-football-10-tips-of-trading-etiquette-strategy/)). Harstad: "if a manager doesn't think a trade will make her team better, she's not going to accept it" ([Footballguys](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2022-dynasty-in-theory-49-how-to-trade)).

3. **Positional need / depth at the asked-for position.** FantasyPros: "Offering a deal that only benefits your team and doesn't address their weaknesses is unlikely to be accepted" ([FantasyPros, beginner's guide](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/06/beginners-guide-understanding-fantasy-football-trades/)); the PFF "two scenarios" where trades happen: positional imbalance in opposite directions, or a genuine value disagreement ([PFF Part 1](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-1/)). RSJ: "Find a manager in the opposite situation. A clean exchange helps both sides" ([Roto Street Journal](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2025/11/14/game-theory-trades-how-to-win-the-trade-market-before-the-playoffs/)). The Dak offer violates this at the extreme: the recipient has no depth at the position being asked for, so there is no "opposite situation."

4. **Roster-spot / drop consequences.** Fantasy Footballers tip #7: in uneven trades "consider who gets dropped. This affects whether the deal genuinely helps both parties." PFF's redraft chart: "the side giving up the most players should expect to pay a premium of 5-10% over the stud player's cost" ([PFF redraft chart](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-for-redraft-fantasy-football-leagues)). CBS/SportsLine's Heath Cummings: "I tack on a 10% premium if I'm giving up the top player in a two-for-one trade. For three-for-one deals, I apply a 25% premium" ([SportsLine](https://www.sportsline.com/insiders/fantasy-football-dynasty-trade-chart-week-13/)). Draft Sharks calls this "Trade Tax": "Lopsided trades (2-for-1, 3-for-1, 3-for-2, etc.) require an overpayment – or tax – from the side giving more players," estimated by an algorithm "that strips away throw-in players" ([Draft Sharks](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/dynasty-trade-calculator)). Search summaries also surfaced a "10–15%" and a "+20 value points" rule and "the side with 2 should value about 50% higher," but I could not fetch those primary pages, so treat them as unverified.

5. **Endowment effect / loss aversion on the named player.** The base literature: Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler's mug experiment found willingness-to-accept ≈ 2× willingness-to-pay ($5.75 vs $2.25) ([Kahneman et al. 1990](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Behavioral_Decision_Theory/Kahneman_et_al_1990_Experimental_tests.pdf)); Hobbs & Singh find "strong evidence of the endowment effect" in NBA/NFL/NHL draft-pick trades that persists beyond information asymmetry ([Economic Inquiry 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/ecinqu/v60y2022i4p1929-1942.html)). Applied to fantasy: "the opposing manager will overvalue those players you ask for because he has instinctively inflated the value of those players simply because he owns them" ([Hardball Times](https://tht.fangraphs.com/the-endowment-effect-and-trade-proposals/)); PFF: "the fear of losing something in a trade is typically greater than the excitement over gaining something" ([PFF Part 2](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-2/)). Practical reading: a 1:1 consensus-value match is *not* perceived as fair by the seller; the acquirer must pay a visible premium, and the premium is largest on stars (Chase).

6. **Fairness as judged by a chart/analyzer the recipient trusts.** PFF: "Adapt approach based on opponent tendencies—some rely on league evaluator tools, others on team fandom" ([PFF Part 2](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-2/)). ESPN's own support page: "the most successful trade offers are those that are evenly matched and benefit both parties to the same extent" ([ESPN support](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003850391)). Section 4 covers what the charts do.

7. **Timing: byes, injuries, standings, deadline.** PFF: Week 3 is "when people start to doubt their underperforming studs" ([PFF Part 1](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-1/)). RSJ: sweet spot "Weeks 8–10, depending on your record"; "Teams with playoff confidence trade for depth. Bubble teams trade for upside. Rebuilders trade veterans" ([RSJ](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2025/11/14/game-theory-trades-how-to-win-the-trade-market-before-the-playoffs/)). 4for4: "target trades with teams in the bottom third of standings first… they have a stud or two, but the depth on their team pales," and top teams "rarely trade away their stars" ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2016/w10/gearing-fantasy-playoffs)). Bleacher Report lists the recipient states that make 2-for-1s land: desperate after a star injury, about to hit a bye crunch, a 5-1 team that "lose[s] 2 or 3 in a row and completely panic[s]" ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/394608-master-the-art-of-the-2-for-1-trade)). The Fantasy Footballers: target players "whose bye weeks have passed" and "easy playoff schedules post-week eight" ([Fantasy Footballers, advanced](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/fantasy-football-advanced-trade-strategies)).

8. **Name value / recency bias.** Sell-high targets are players "exceeding their established talent levels"; "players can continue to have more name value than production" ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/fantasy-football-trade-targets-players-to-buy-low-sell-high-97569), [4for4 Trader's Alley](https://www.4for4.com/2022/w3/fantasy-football-week-3-traders-alley-buy-low-sell-high-and-chart)). RSJ's checklist: "Volume predicts future points better than name value." For an offer generator, recency bias cuts the other way: the *recipient* prices your players on last-three-weeks box scores, so an offer built on your model's `mu_ros` will be rejected if the market (chart) value of your side is lower.

9. **QBs in 1QB leagues have little trade value.** "QBs generally hold the least positional trade value in 1QB redraft leagues… quarterbacks are generally harder to trade due to a lack of positional need" ([RSJ chart](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2025/11/17/2025-fantasy-football-week-12-trade-value-chart/)); PFF: "there are five other quarterbacks on the waiver wire who could put up top-10 numbers" ([PFF Part 2](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-2/)). FantasyCalc notes the exception: for elite QBs "three-for-one trades are most common," which is why they carry high values ([FantasyCalc FAQ via search](https://fantasycalc.com/frequently-asked-questions)). So Montgomery-for-Dak fails twice: Dak is his only QB (need), and a 1QB-league QB is normally *cheap* to buy only when the seller has a second starter.

10. **Who to send to.** Footballguys: "Target Active Traders… scour their rosters first for a player to target" ([Footballguys life hacks](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2024-life-hacks-for-improved-trading-skills)); PFF tip #3 "Study people's personalities and trading tendencies" and #15 "Vulture the deal"; Fantasy Footballers tip #1 "Don't be that owner that blindly shoots out a trade offer to every single team."

11. **Offer volume and the "focusing illusion."** Baseball Prospectus: "each offer increases our chances of setting the focus of the negotiation," but "send more offers does not mean… egregiously one-sided offers," and offers that "demonstrate an ability to understand the recipient's needs" have a higher success rate at least for continued negotiation ([Baseball Prospectus](https://baseballprospectus.com/fantasy/article/31232/the-quinton-sending-and-receiving-trade-offers-and-the-focusing-illusion)). Lowballs have a cost: "a trade offer that is too clearly in your favor puts you in danger of potential trade partners shutting you out… for any future ones as well" ([RSJ chart](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2025/11/17/2025-fantasy-football-week-12-trade-value-chart/)).

## 2. Game-theory framing

**Where the surplus comes from.** Trades are positive-sum only when the two lineups value the same player differently, and the documented sources of that are: (a) **lineup-slot arbitrage** — "trading two bench pieces for one elite starter is almost always a win… because bench points don't win matchups" ([sticktothemodel VORP](https://sticktothemodel.com/blog/fantasy-football-vorp-explained-2025)); the Yahoo trade-evaluator patent formalizes this by computing each team's optimal lineup before/after and reporting "the total delta in points for each team," with bench slots contributing zero ([US8340794](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8340794)); (b) **positional scarcity / replacement level** — PFF: "We assign a value of zero to players who are just out of the realm of possible starts… every player above replacement is then assigned a value based on the rest-of-season projections" ([PFF chart](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-for-redraft-fantasy-football-leagues)); RB elite-vs-waiver gap ≈ 13 ppg vs WR ≈ 9.5 ppg per one VORP explainer (unverified numbers, [search summary](https://walterpicks.beehiiv.com/p/vorp-the-key-to-dominating-your-fantasy-football-drafts)); (c) **roster-spot value** — FantasyCalc adds "a small value… to consider the value of a bench spot, and it increases with each additional bench spot needed to complete the trade" ([FantasyCalc FAQ via search](https://fantasycalc.com/frequently-asked-questions)); RedraftCalc lets you "add free agents to either side of a trade to see how available pickups change the deal's fairness" ([RedraftCalc](https://www.redraftcalc.com/)); (d) **schedule/bye/playoff complementarity** ([Fantasy Footballers advanced](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/fantasy-football-advanced-trade-strategies), [4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2016/w10/gearing-fantasy-playoffs)); (e) **objective mismatch** (contender vs seller, below). Harstad's dynasty framing applies to redraft contenders too: "win-now trades… are trades where you are receiving less total value than you're giving up, but you're making that sacrifice to increase your chances in some narrow window" ([Footballguys](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2023-dynasty-in-theory-65-chase-value-not-wins)).

**Negotiation dynamics.** Anchoring: "make your first offer at about 75-90% of the maximum you are willing to give up" ([Fantasy Footballers advanced](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/fantasy-football-advanced-trade-strategies)); "The initial offer should be tilted in your favor; there's no reason to give unnecessary concessions" but not so far that leaguemates disengage ([Fantasy Footballers negotiation](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/analysis/negotiation-strategy-five-tips-for-your-fantasy-football-trades/)); theScore: "Never lead with your best offer." Counter-view from PFF: "Avoid inflated player values in opening offers, as this torpedoes negotiations" and, with unresponsive traders, "I'll send a solid offer with little-to-no comments" ([PFF Part 2](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-2/)). Framing: emphasize what the recipient *gains* ([Hardball Times](https://tht.fangraphs.com/the-endowment-effect-and-trade-proposals/)); "ask why they would be willing to make this trade? How does this trade help them?" ([jonsalve](https://jonsalve.substack.com/p/fantasy-football-2024-how-to-trade)). Structure: "It's hard to find a perfect value match in a one-for-one swap. Add another pair of players to tweak the value" (PFF tip #13); jonsalve's structural trick: your best player + your worst bench asset for two of their mid-tier starters, because "the downgrade from giving up a mid-range starter for a lesser mid-range starter isn't as significant as the upgrade," and "Trying to swindle someone on a 3-for-1 deal isn't realistic."

**Sell depth / buy the star vs buy depth from a contender.** Both are in the sources, keyed on the counterparty's standing: contenders consolidate ("Trade two solid mid-tier players for one elite starter"), bubble teams buy upside, sellers in the bottom third accept 2-for-1s for their stud because their "depth pales" ([RSJ](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2025/11/14/game-theory-trades-how-to-win-the-trade-market-before-the-playoffs/), [4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2016/w10/gearing-fantasy-playoffs)). The Chase offer is a consolidation pitched at (presumably) a contender; consolidation offers only clear when the recipient is the one with thin starting slots.

**Win probability vs expected points.** Skinner (JQAS 2011): "an underdog must be willing to adopt a strategy whose outcome has a larger-than-average variance," and the paper quantifies "how much the team should be willing to sacrifice from its mean score in order to increase the score's variance" ([arXiv 1111.0693](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0693)). Footballguys: "For the favorite, variance is the enemy… For the underdog, variance is a friend" ([Footballguys](https://www.footballguys.com/article/DFS_expectationvariance)). Bales shows a stacked roster (62.5% of starters on two NFL teams) has "little upside" because teammates' production is inversely correlated, which hurts an underdog and is tolerable for a favorite ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/according-to-the-data-having-too-many-players-on-one-team-18571)). Harstad gives the scale of weekly noise: CV "between 0.35 and 0.65… 68% of the time, a player scores within +/- 50% of his weekly projection" ([Footballguys](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2023-dynasty-in-theory-83-skill-vs-luck)). Consequence: a 2-for-1 that raises a contender's mean but concentrates variance in one player can be right for a favorite and wrong for a team that needs to run the table; a packet objective of "+ppw for me" should be replaced (or gated) by playoff-odds delta. I found no published tool that scores trades on playoff probability for redraft; the only "win probability" numbers in search results came from content-farm pages and should not be cited.

## 3. Heuristics an offer generator should encode

Each with its source basis; thresholds marked (rule of thumb) are analyst conventions, not measured acceptance data.

1. **Never ask for a player the counterparty has no replacement for at that slot.** Compute his post-trade optimal lineup (Yahoo-patent style); reject any package where a starting slot becomes EMPTY or is filled by a sub-replacement body. Explicitly: never ask for a manager's only QB/TE/K/DST in a 1-of-each league ([PFF Part 1](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-1/), [Harstad](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2022-dynasty-in-theory-49-how-to-trade)).
2. **The counterparty's projected starting-lineup points must not go down** (strict: must go up at his weakest slot). "If your offer moves points from their starting lineup to their bench, then don't bother" ([PFF](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-1/)). Target his weakest starting slot by projection and deliver the upgrade there ([FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/06/beginners-guide-understanding-fantasy-football-trades/)).
3. **Give the best player in the deal, or pay a visible premium when you take him.** Operationalize as: market value of best player on my side ≥ best player on his side, OR my total market value ≥ his by the consolidation tax. Taxes in print: 5–10% (PFF redraft), 10% for 2-for-1 / 25% for 3-for-1 (Cummings/CBS), "trade tax" with throw-ins stripped (Draft Sharks). A sensible default is ≥ +10% on a 2-for-1, ≥ +25% on a 3-for-1, after dropping throw-ins ([PFF](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-for-redraft-fantasy-football-leagues), [SportsLine](https://www.sportsline.com/insiders/fantasy-football-dynasty-trade-chart-week-13/), [Draft Sharks](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/dynasty-trade-calculator)).
4. **Add the endowment premium on top for stars.** WTA/WTP ≈ 2× in lab settings ([KKT 1990](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Behavioral_Decision_Theory/Kahneman_et_al_1990_Experimental_tests.pdf)); in practice, do not target a top-3 player at a position unless the package is ≥ market value + tax *and* fills his weakest slot. Prefer tier-2/3 targets where the ask reads as lateral.
5. **Fairness band on consensus value.** sticktothemodel: "Inside 5% is a fair trade" ([sticktothemodel](https://sticktothemodel.com/fantasy/trade)); FantasyCalc discards trades "that have large value differences between the two sides" as outliers (threshold unpublished). Use a market chart the recipient can check (FantasyCalc redraft, KTC redraft, FantasyPros/ESPN analyzer) and require my-side ≥ his-side − 5% on 1-for-1s, ≥ +tax on uneven packages.
6. **Roster cost of uneven deals.** Charge the side receiving more bodies the value of the player he must drop (Yahoo chart's rule: include "the values for the players you'd be moving to the bench or dropping," search summary of [AOL/Yahoo chart](https://www.aol.com/articles/fantasy-football-week-8-trade-115950505.html), page not fetchable); credit the side receiving fewer bodies with the best free agent at the vacated slot ([RedraftCalc](https://www.redraftcalc.com/), FantasyCalc bench-spot value). Your existing `_Spots`/`position_limits` logic already tracks the drop; apply it to *his* roster too.
7. **Offer only players at least one tier above his waiver wire** (PFF tip #10) — a bench WR as the second piece in the Chase offer is a throw-in by this test, and your `throw_ins` rule should price it at ~0 on *his* side, which it already does; the generator should then refuse to send a package whose stripped value fails rule 3.
8. **Prefer 1-for-1 and 2-for-2; treat 2-for-1 as a special case requiring a seller.** jonsalve: 1-for-1 / 2-for-2 "have a higher success rate"; RSJ/4for4: 2-for-1 consolidation only to depth-poor, bottom-third, injury-hit or bye-crunched teams; contenders buy depth.
9. **Score counterparty state:** record/standing (contender / bubble / seller), injuries at the position, upcoming byes, playoff schedule, and whether he has traded before this season (ESPN's transaction log exposes this). Weight active traders and desperate states up ([Footballguys life hacks](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2024-life-hacks-for-improved-trading-skills), [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/394608-master-the-art-of-the-2-for-1-trade)).
10. **QB rule for 1QB leagues:** only ask for a QB from a roster with 2+ startable QBs, and expect to pay little in market value but to solve a *need* on his side; never send an RB1/WR1 for a QB ([RSJ](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2025/11/17/2025-fantasy-football-week-12-trade-value-chart/), [PFF](https://www.pff.com/trade-strategy-2/)).
11. **Anchor modestly:** open at 75–90% of your walk-away, with a pre-computed sweetener (your cheapest above-replacement bench piece) to concede ([Fantasy Footballers](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/fantasy-football-advanced-trade-strategies)). Do not lowball — reputation cost compounds across the season ([RSJ](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2025/11/17/2025-fantasy-football-week-12-trade-value-chart/), [Fantasy Footballers 10 tips](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/fantasy-football-10-tips-of-trading-etiquette-strategy/)).
12. **Objective:** rank packages by my playoff-odds (or win-prob) delta, not ppw alone, with a variance term signed by my standing ([Skinner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0693), [Footballguys](https://www.footballguys.com/article/DFS_expectationvariance)).
13. **Message:** one line stating the slot it fixes for him and the bye/injury it covers ([Berry via search](https://africa.espn.com/ffl/story/_/page/TMRlovehate191003/matthew-berry-love-hate-fantasy-football-picks-sleepers-busts-week-5-how-trade), [jonsalve](https://jonsalve.substack.com/p/fantasy-football-2024-how-to-trade)). (The project is read-only against ESPN, so this is text for Dustin to paste.)

## 4. How the analyzers and charts work, and what managers compare against

- **ESPN Trade Grades (IBM watsonx, since 2023).** Per-player grade plus overall trade grade, shown in the trade flow with three LLM-written "Top Contributing Factors." Grades are "the relative benefit a player from an opponent's roster would add to your team," computed by "a rules-based system in combination with a plurality of machine learning models," using inputs like "How many leagues own this player, what percentage of leagues start the player, what are their projected seasonal stats, who have they played, who will they play," and comparing "a potential player's grade to the average grade of players in the same position already on your roster." A "team needs" app recomputes per-user grades every 10 minutes ([IBM case study](https://www.ibm.com/case-studies/blog/fantasy-football-trades-how-ibm-granite-foundation-models-drive-personalized-explainability-for-millions), [IBM 2023 release](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2023-09-13-IBM-Brings-watsonx-to-ESPN-Fantasy-Football-with-New-Waiver-Grades-and-Trade-Grades)). Since your leagues are ESPN, this is the grade every recipient sees before clicking Accept: it is **roster-relative** (position average on *his* roster) and leans on start% / ownership, i.e., name value. An offer that sends him a player below his positional average grades poorly regardless of your model's `mu_ros`.
- **Yahoo.** Patented evaluator computes optimal lineups before/after and shows each team's point delta (bench = 0) ([US8340794](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8340794)); Yahoo Fantasy Plus "Trade Hub" adds team strengths/weaknesses and "Top 3 trade partners… matched with other teams in your league" ([Yahoo help](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN35629.html)).
- **Sleeper.** No native analyzer found; KTC values are injected via a browser extension into "player popups, the matchup tab… and trades tab" ([search summary](https://extscope.org/extension/iplmcdkdjgaaglaianjppbecajjnghcf)).
- **FantasyCalc.** Values "algorithmically generated from" ~7.65M real trades, refreshed every 3 hours, via an optimization over the trade database; outlier trades removed; recency-weighted; adjusted for SF/TEP/PPR/team count; bench-spot value added per extra roster spot; elite QBs priced off 3-for-1 patterns ([FantasyCalc FAQ via search](https://fantasycalc.com/frequently-asked-questions)). The public API (`api.fantasycalc.com/values/current?isDynasty=false&numQbs=1&numTeams=12&ppr=1`) returns per-player `value`, `redraftValue`, `overallRank`, `positionRank`, `trend30Day`, `maybeTradeFrequency` on a ~0–10,500 scale (today: #1 ≈ 10.6k, #10 ≈ 8.0k, #50 ≈ 2.5k, #100 ≈ 0.85k; top QB ≈ 6.0k, QB3 ≈ 2.5k). This is the cheapest "what the market thinks" feed for the generator.
- **KeepTradeCut.** Crowdsourced via Keep/Trade/Cut votes ("more than 26 million datapoints," ELO-based, updated every 10 minutes) plus "tradesourced" values from real trades; separate redraft 1QB/SF databases through Week 16; the calculator's "Value Adjustment" "add[s] value to the side of the trade that's giving up more when you look at roster spots, players' 'stud' factor," scaled by "the number of players of lesser value included" ([KTC about](https://keeptradecut.com/about/player-values), [KTC FAQ](https://keeptradecut.com/frequently-asked-questions)). This is the formalization of the consolidation tax most managers have seen.
- **FantasyPros.** ECR + ROS projections, league-synced, "weighted score" verdict; Trade Finder scans rosters ([FantasyPros support](https://support.fantasypros.com/hc/en-us/articles/26338614597915-What-Tools-Do-You-Have-for-Trade-Help) – page returned 403; description via [CheatSheet War Room roundup](https://www.cheatsheetwarroom.com/blog/fantasy-football/tools/trade-analyzer-calculator)).
- **Value-over-baseline charts.** FantasyFootballCalculator: "the projected fantasy points of the last starter for each position is subtracted" from ROS projections ([FFC](https://fantasyfootballcalculator.com/trade-analyzer)); PFF: replacement = "just out of the realm of possible starts." RotoWire's 2026 analyzer is roster-aware ("weighs the trade against the starters and bench each team actually has") and filters deals that "gut the other team" ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/fantasy-football-trade-analyzer-find-and-grade-redraft-fantasy-football-trades-in-2026-134904)). RotoTrade adds a "Package Adjustment" and a "Chance the other team accepts" percentage, but publishes no method ([RotoTrade](https://www.rototrade.com/fantasy-football-trade-analyzer)).
- **Relationship between chart values and acceptance:** no published data. The closest proxies are FantasyCalc's outlier filter (trades far off chart value are rare enough to be dropped) and KTC's note that market ("revealed preference") values are more stable than crowd ("stated preference") values. Treat "within 5% on a 1-for-1, +10%/+25% tax on 2-for-1/3-for-1, and recipient's ESPN grade not negative" as the best-available acceptance gate.
